What we need from you

Four things that make a build succeed

  • 01A person who owns the processSomeone who can answer “what actually happens when X is missing?” and approve the rules.
  • 02Access to the systems involvedCRM, mailbox, sheets, messaging accounts — scoped to the minimum the workflow needs.
  • 03A week of real volumeRecent examples, so the build is tested against what actually arrives and not what we imagine.
  • 04Willingness to change the processAutomation that mirrors a broken process just makes the broken part faster.
Commitments we make

Working principles, in writing

Business-first automationWe build because a specific process is costing time or money. If a workflow does not justify automation, we say so.
Practical AIUseful systems, not AI for hype. Language models do the understanding; deterministic rules do the things that must not drift.
Connected systemsAutomation is only as good as its integrations. We work inside the tools you already run rather than selling you a new one.
Human oversightWhere judgement, money or trust is involved, a person stays in the loop. The system prepares work; it does not secretly approve it.
Scalable architectureDesigned so ten times the volume means more throughput, not more breakage, and so the next workflow is easier than the first.
Continuous improvementSystems drift as businesses change. We review logs and override patterns, then tighten the design over time.
